# What Is a Service-Level Agreement (SLA)

A service-level agreement (SLA) is a documented commitment between a
service provider and a customer that defines the expected level of
service, typically expressed as measurable targets such as uptime
percentage, response time, or resolution time.

## Common SLA Metrics

Frequent SLA metrics include availability (often expressed as "three
nines," 99.9% uptime, or higher), first-response time for a support
request, and time-to-resolution for incidents of a given severity.
Availability targets are usually measured over a rolling window, such as
a calendar month.

## Severity-Based Response Targets

Many SLAs tier their response and resolution targets by incident
severity: a full outage typically carries the tightest response-time
commitment, while a minor cosmetic issue carries a much longer one. This
lets a provider allocate urgency proportionally to customer impact rather
than treating every report identically.

## SLA Credits and Remedies

When a provider misses an SLA target, the agreement often specifies a
remedy, commonly a service credit proportional to the severity and
duration of the miss. SLA credits are a contractual remedy, not a
substitute for actually fixing the underlying reliability problem that
caused the miss.
