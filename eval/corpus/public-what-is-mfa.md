# What Is Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) requires a user to present two or more
independent pieces of evidence, called factors, to prove their identity
before being granted access to a system. This is stronger than a
password alone, which is a single factor that can be stolen, guessed, or
reused across sites.

## The Three Factor Categories

Authentication factors generally fall into three categories: something
you know (a password or PIN), something you have (a phone, hardware
security key, or authenticator app), and something you are (a
fingerprint or other biometric). MFA combines factors from at least two
of these categories.

## Common MFA Methods

Common MFA methods include time-based one-time codes generated by an
authenticator app, push notifications sent to a registered device asking
the user to approve or deny a sign-in attempt, and physical hardware
security keys that use public-key cryptography to prove possession of
the key without transmitting a shared secret.

## Why MFA Matters

Because a stolen or leaked password alone is not enough to sign in when
MFA is enabled, MFA substantially reduces the impact of common attacks
such as password reuse, phishing of a plain password, and credential-
stuffing attempts that try leaked username/password pairs against many
services.
