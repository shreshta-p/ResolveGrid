internal security review as insufficient for a remote-access system with
access to production infrastructure. See the v2 policy for the current
rules, which shortened the rotation window, made MFA mandatory, and moved
password resets to a self-service flow.

# Kestrel VPN Access Policy (v2)

This is the **current** Kestrel Corp remote-access VPN policy, effective
2025-06-01. It supersedes "Kestrel VPN Access Policy (v1, deprecated)" in
full. All employees connecting to internal systems remotely must follow
these rules.

## Client Software

Install "KestrelConnect" (current major version, distributed through the
internal software portal) on any device used for remote access. The
legacy "KestrelConnect Classic" client is no longer supported and will be
blocked from connecting after its end-of-life date.

## Password Reset

To reset your VPN password, open the KestrelConnect client and select
"Forgot Password" on the sign-in screen. This sends a one-time reset link
to your Kestrel email address; the self-service flow completes in
minutes, replacing the old email-the-Security-team process. If you no
longer have access to your Kestrel email, contact the IT Support desk for
identity verification.

## Password and MFA Rules

VPN passwords must be at least 12 characters and are rotated every 90
days. Multi-factor authentication (a push notification to the Kestrel
Authenticator app) is mandatory for every VPN session, with no
opt-out, for all employees and contractors.

## Access Scope

VPN access grants routes only to the internal systems a given role
requires (least-privilege routing), not a flat connection to the entire
corporate network. Access scope is tied to your department and any
project-specific grants your manager has approved.

# Kestrel Laptop Provisioning and Offboarding Policy

This policy covers how the IT Support team issues, images, and reclaims
Kestrel Corp laptops for employees and contractors.

## New Hire Provisioning

IT Support images a new laptop with the standard Kestrel software bundle
(OS, endpoint security agent, KestrelConnect VPN client, and
department-specific tooling) before an employee's start date. New hires
pick up their laptop from the Austin HQ IT desk, or receive it by courier
for remote hires, no later than their first day.

## Loaner Devices

A loaner laptop is available from the IT desk for employees whose primary
device is being repaired. Loaners are pre-imaged with the same standard
bundle and must be returned within 10 business days or the request must
be renewed with a manager's approval.

## Offboarding and Device Return

When an employee leaves Kestrel Corp, IT Support disables their device's
VPN and single sign-on access on their last working day. The laptop must
be returned to IT Support (in person or by prepaid courier for remote
employees) within 5 business days of departure. Devices not returned
within 30 days are reported to Security as a potential asset-recovery
issue.

## Support Requests

For laptop hardware issues, software installation requests, or questions
about this policy, open a ticket with the IT Support queue rather than
contacting individual technicians directly, so requests are tracked and
prioritized consistently.

# Kestrel Platform Engineering On-Call Escalation Policy

This policy defines how production incidents are escalated within the
Platform Engineering department.

## On-Call Rotation

Platform Engineering maintains a weekly on-call rotation. The primary
on-call engineer is the first responder for every paging alert; a
secondary on-call engineer is paged automatically if the primary does not
acknowledge within 10 minutes.

## Severity Levels and Response Times

Sev-1 incidents (full service outage) require acknowledgment within 5
minutes and an initial status update within 15 minutes. Sev-2 incidents
(significant degradation, partial outage) require acknowledgment within
15 minutes. Sev-3 incidents (minor, non-customer-facing issues) are
handled during normal business hours and do not page the on-call
rotation.

## Escalation Path

If a Sev-1 incident is not mitigated within 30 minutes, the on-call
engineer escalates to the Platform Engineering duty manager, who can pull
in additional engineers or declare a formal incident bridge. Incidents
touching authentication or data integrity are escalated to Security in
parallel, regardless of severity level.

## Postmortems

Every Sev-1 and Sev-2 incident requires a written postmortem within 5
business days, covering root cause, timeline, and follow-up action items.
Postmortems are blameless and are reviewed in the department's weekly
engineering sync.

# Kestrel People Ops Time-Off Request Policy

This policy explains how Kestrel Corp employees request and track paid
time off (PTO) through People Ops.

## Requesting Time Off

Submit a time-off request through the internal HR portal at least 5
business days before the requested start date for planned leave. Requests
under 3 days are auto-approved once your manager acknowledges the
request; longer requests require explicit manager sign-off before they
are confirmed.

## PTO Accrual

Full-time employees accrue PTO at a fixed rate per pay period based on
tenure, up to an annual cap. Unused PTO up to 40 hours carries over into
the next calendar year; any balance above that is forfeited at year-end
unless local law requires otherwise.

## Sick Leave and Emergency Leave

Sick leave is tracked separately from PTO and does not require advance
notice, only notification to your manager as soon as practical. Emergency
leave requests (bereavement, family emergencies) are handled the same
way; People Ops can backfill the paperwork after the fact.

## Questions and Disputes

Direct questions about accrual balances, denied requests, or policy
exceptions to the People Ops queue rather than to your manager directly,
so there is a consistent record of how exceptions are handled.

# What Is a VPN

A virtual private network (VPN) is a technology that creates an encrypted
connection, often called a tunnel, between a device and a private
network over the public internet. Traffic sent through the tunnel is
encrypted, so an observer on the intervening network path sees only
opaque encrypted packets rather than the underlying data.

## Why Organizations Use VPNs

Organizations commonly use VPNs to let remote employees reach internal
systems, such as file servers or internal applications, as though they
were physically on the corporate network. A VPN client authenticates the
user (often combined with multi-factor authentication) before granting
access to the tunnel.

## Common VPN Protocols

Widely used VPN protocols include IPsec, OpenVPN, and WireGuard. They
differ in performance characteristics and cryptographic design, but all
serve the same basic purpose: authenticating a connection and encrypting
the traffic that flows through it.

## Split Tunneling

Some VPN configurations use "split tunneling," where only traffic bound
for the private network goes through the encrypted tunnel and other
internet traffic goes directly out the user's normal connection. This
improves performance for general browsing but must be configured
carefully so sensitive traffic is not accidentally routed outside the
tunnel.

# What Is Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) requires a user to present two or more
independent pieces of evidence, called factors, to prove their identity
before being granted access to a system. This is stronger than a
password alone, which is a single factor that can be stolen, guessed, or
reused across sites.

## The Three Factor Categories

Authentication factors generally fall into three categories: something
you know (a password or PIN), something you have (a phone, hardware
security key, or authenticator app), and something you are (a
fingerprint or other biometric). MFA combines factors from at least two
of these categories.

## Common MFA Methods

Common MFA methods include time-based one-time codes generated by an
authenticator app, push notifications sent to a registered device asking
the user to approve or deny a sign-in attempt, and physical hardware
security keys that use public-key cryptography to prove possession of
the key without transmitting a shared secret.

## Why MFA Matters

Because a stolen or leaked password alone is not enough to sign in when
MFA is enabled, MFA substantially reduces the impact of common attacks
such as password reuse, phishing of a plain password, and credential-
stuffing attempts that try leaked username/password pairs against many
services.

# What Is a Service-Level Agreement (SLA)

A service-level agreement (SLA) is a documented commitment between a
service provider and a customer that defines the expected level of
service, typically expressed as measurable targets such as uptime
percentage, response time, or resolution time.

## Common SLA Metrics

Frequent SLA metrics include availability (often expressed as "three
nines," 99.9% uptime, or higher), first-response time for a support
request, and time-to-resolution for incidents of a given severity.
Availability targets are usually measured over a rolling window, such as
a calendar month.

## Severity-Based Response Targets

Many SLAs tier their response and resolution targets by incident
severity: a full outage typically carries the tightest response-time
commitment, while a minor cosmetic issue carries a much longer one. This
lets a provider allocate urgency proportionally to customer impact rather
than treating every report identically.

## SLA Credits and Remedies

When a provider misses an SLA target, the agreement often specifies a
remedy, commonly a service credit proportional to the severity and
duration of the miss. SLA credits are a contractual remedy, not a
substitute for actually fixing the underlying reliability problem that
caused the miss.
